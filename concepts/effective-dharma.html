How we rank

Our methodology is fully transparent. We convert every claimed outcome into Liberation-Adjusted Life Years (LALYs), discount for reporting bias, then divide by cost. The remainder is your practice.

Moral weights

Comparing outcomes across retreats requires a common unit. We surveyed 12 practitioners on how they would trade off different mental states. Eleven were asleep. The twelfth said the question dissolves on inspection. We have used the twelfth’s answer for all weights.

Counterfactual impact

We only credit a retreat for insights you would not otherwise have had. Most insights, on review, were available at home for free. We adjust accordingly.

Reporting bias

Retreatants systematically over-report attainments in the week after a retreat and systematically under-report them six months later, when they have stopped talking about it. We use the six-month figure.

Room for more sitting

Some centres are capacity-constrained: an additional retreatant displaces another retreatant, producing no net LALYs. We prefer interventions where your marginal hour is genuinely additional. Your own chair scores perfectly on this axis, which is a problem for the retreat sector and we have said so.

Cause prioritization

Not all suffering is equally tractable. Using our importance–neglectedness–tractability framework, we have identified the cause areas where an additional hour of attention does the most good.

Long-sitterism

The vast majority of your moments have not happened yet. If you weight future suffering equally with present suffering, this cause dominates every other consideration. Critics say this is a reason to worry more. We say it is a reason to sit longer.

Priority: Highest

Rebirth risk

A small chance of an astronomically long sequence of lives implies an astronomically large expected value from ending the sequence. We are neutral on whether rebirth is real; the expected-value math works either way, which we find suspicious but not disqualifying.

Priority: High, if real

Wild-animal jhana

Trillions of animals may be experiencing states of concentration and have no framework for reporting them. This is likely the largest unmeasured contemplative population on Earth. Highly neglected. Tractability unknown.

Priority: Speculative

Our mistakes

We think it is important to be honest about where we have gone wrong. This page is updated whenever we notice, which our methodology suggests is the point.

  1. 2019We ranked a retreat highly on the strength of a single glowing testimonial. The testimonial was the teacher’s.
  2. 2020We rated every online retreat as “highly tractable” without adjusting for the presence of a Leave Meeting button. Effect sizes were revised down by 60%.
  3. 2021We built a cost-effectiveness model for a Zen retreat and sent it to the centre for comment. They replied that the model was “exactly the problem.” We have listed this as a limitation.
  4. 2022We double-counted the counterfactual. Every retreat was credited for the insights you would have had anyway, and then debited for them, and then credited again. Rankings were unchanged, which worried us more.
  5. 2023A senior researcher left mid-evaluation to ordain. We have recorded this as a positive outcome for the retreat and a negative outcome for headcount, and we are unsure which column is which.
  6. 2024We omitted the Silent Hike control condition from the published rankings. When restored, it outperformed nine of the fourteen retreats. We received letters.
  7. 2025Our moral-weights survey was administered on day nine of a ten-day course. Responses included “yes,” “no,” and a drawing of the dining hall.
  8. 2026We attempted to compute the moral weight of a single moment of awareness. The spreadsheet has not been the same since, and neither has the analyst.
  9. OngoingWe continue to assume there is someone reading this page who will benefit from it. We have not been able to locate them.
